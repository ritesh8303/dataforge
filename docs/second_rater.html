Information for participants

You are invited to provide independent relevance labels for job–seeker pairs. These labels support inter-rater agreement analysis in a design-science thesis on semantic vs keyword job matching (DataForge).

Purpose

  • Compare ranking methods under hard constraints (skills, seniority, language, location, visa).
  • Your grades are compared with the primary author’s labels (agreement / disagreement).

What you will do

  • Read the short person story at the top (their real situation), then the job below.
  • Ask: would this job work for that person? Tap 0–3 (auto-advances). 40 items, ~12–18 min.
  • Expand the JD only if needed. Hard limits in their story (city, visa, internship, language) beat a fancy title.

Coding scale

0 — No fit Clear mismatch, or hard constraint fails.
1 — Weak Partial overlap; major gaps remain.
2 — Good Plausible match with minor gaps.
3 — Excellent Strong match on role and applicable rules.

Hard constraints (seniority, language, location, visa): if the ad clearly fails the stated rule, assign 0 even if skills look related.

Missing evidence: visa/sponsorship and remote are often not written in the JD (sometimes only on the application form). Judge from the text shown here only. If the hard rule cannot be verified, tick Unclear and grade 0 for that constraint — do not assume a pass.

Modern JDs ask for many tools. Grade on the seeker’s stated strengths and the tip — do not require a perfect all-rounder.

Data use & privacy

  • Collected: your name, optional email, timestamps, and grades.
  • Use: thesis appendix / methods section (rater attribution) and agreement statistics.
  • Full job descriptions are shown (scrollable). Open the original posting only if a link is missing or text looks incomplete.
  • Participation is voluntary; you may stop at any time. Progress is stored only in this browser until you submit.
  • On submit, answers are emailed to the researcher and a JSON copy is downloaded to your device.

Investigator: Ritesh Jadhav · M.Sc. Data Science thesis · DataForge case study. Contact via the email you may optionally leave, or the invitation message.
